# V3 — planner's notes for the report writer (mechanisms, decisions, trade-offs)

*Written by the planning model on 2026-09-12 after the two engineering cycles. The report writer takes every NUMBER from the final `bench/out/*.json`, `data/processed/calibration.json` and `data/processed/meta_*.json`; this file supplies the mechanisms, the decisions and their reasons. Where this file and the JSON disagree on a number, the JSON wins.*

## 1. What V3 changed (by priority of the brief)

1. **First-stop prior (Priority 1).** `src/firststop.py` builds a per-circuit density over the green-flag first-stop lap from the circuit's 2023-25 races (Gaussian KDE, bandwidth 2.5 laps at 60 laps, 5 % uniform floor), scaled to the race distance and **conditioned on the start compound (through the Pirelli nominations) and on the plan's stop count** (a first-stop distribution per family, the analogue of the plan-shape prior). It enters every objective as `kappa * (-log p)` seconds with the modal lap at zero: `strategy.simulate_model` (phase 1 and 2), `pit_window_model`, `evaluate_plans`, `counterfactual` (green-flag first stops), `per_driver_plans`, the outlook and the live engine (first-stint cars only). κ is calibrated leave-one-out in `scripts/80_recalibrate.py` (grid 0-6 s/nat, objective = mean |first stop − field green median| on the non-SC donor weekends, iterated with λ and τ). A family the circuit's field has never run (a one-stop at Melbourne) carries no penalty. **The undercut-exposure term λ was calibrated to 0 in every block once κ entered** (V2: 0.1-0.3): the two terms compete for the same objective and the prior wins it.
2. **Plan prior through Pirelli C-numbers (Priority 2).** `src/nominations.py` + `data/pirelli_nominations.json` (verified nominations 2023-26). Rules: identical nomination → letters; shifted (≥2 shared C-numbers) → every stint mapped by C-number; a mapped plan that collapses onto one compound (Melbourne 2023's M-H = C3-C2 → H-H) contributes its stop count only; disjoint/unknown → stop counts only, then the 2026 season pool mapped by C-number, then role letters (flagged). Only Barcelona (2023, 2025) and Melbourne 2023 change; Barcelona's history becomes M-H-H modal (10/36) with 31 MEDIUM starts. The season pool for a new circuit (Madring) is mapped too — see §5.
3. **Regime factor (Priority 3).** `regime_prior` applies the thermal term only with a supplied forecast (`40_weekend.py --race-temp`); otherwise the donors' raw log ratios are pooled with the median, and a **circuit-level practice→race prior** from the circuit's own 2023-25 practice sessions and races (`src/regime_history.py`, `scripts/05_history_practice.py`, 24 sessions fetched once and cached) is combined with precision weights (ln-sd = sqrt(max(between-year spread, 0.20)² + 0.15² tyre-generation term)). Six of seven circuits have one (Melbourne's only dry year is out of band). Mean absolute log error vs each weekend's self-measured ratio: V2 0.36 → donor median 0.22 → with the circuit prior 0.19.
4. **Cliff detector + censored budgets (Priority 4).** `src/cliff.py`: a within-stint hinge fit on fuel- and evolution-corrected race laps calls a stint `collapse` (RSS drop ≥ 25 %, slope break ≥ 0.15 s/lap, last three laps ≥ 0.6 s off trend, stint ends within 3 laps of the knee), `strategic` (last three laps within ±0.4 s of trend) or `undetermined`. Across the seven races only 4 of 339 long stints collapse; the rest are right-censored lower bounds on the budget. The estimator is a censored log-normal MLE with the config prior; a bound can only raise the estimate (cycle-2 correction: V2's "any bound ≥ 3.0 s is the estimate" handed a held-out Barcelona a 3.27 s MEDIUM from another circuit's bound, below the prior), and a collapse observation below the median bound of its compound is excluded as not a tyre cliff (the four sit at 0.9-2.5 s against >100 stints that ran past 3-4 s on trend). Net effect on budgets: small (global S/M/H ≈ 3.84/4.09/4.3 vs V2 3.80/3.83/3.84). The live wear-based cliff alarm is retired: `cliff_alarm` now means the within-stint pace-collapse detector on the car's own laps, beside the pit-window and new box-now alerts.
5. **Per-car (Priority 5).** `src/percar.py`: team-pooled practice deviations (own weight n/(n+20 laps)), historical race factors shrunk by their ln-sd; the live likelihood and continuation cost use the car's pooled deviation. Measured honestly in `bench_accuracy` as per-car rate scalings: **none of the within-weekend variants improves held-out stint ranking** (Spearman ≈ −0.02/−0.03 vs +0.04 for the field curve, +0.05 for V2's LOO factors). Per-car plans still use the pooled deviations (shipped `percar_mode = team_pooled`); the report must say the evidence does not support them as a ranking tool.
6. **Secondary.** Dirty air per circuit from the circuit's own 2023-25 races (`CircuitPrior.dirty_air`, precision-weighted, never the target's 2026 race): Budapest +0.41, Suzuka +0.23, Spa +0.21, Spielberg +0.14, Melbourne +0.13, Barcelona +0.12, Monza +0.10 — all positive; the negative 2026 measurements at Suzuka/Monza are not reproduced by their histories, and the leave-one-out rule forbids using them. Melbourne ladder rule: pre-2026 net-step measurements with se > 0.15 s dropped, survivors weighted by recency (2025 1.0 / 2024 0.7 / 2023 0.5) — Australia's pooled net −0.066 → +0.073 and its ladder gate passes, but the same rule drops all three Budapest history nets, Hungary's pooled net rises 0.064 → 0.131 (self-measured −0.01) and the enforced ladder gate re-prices the HARD, which flips Hungary from M-H-H (32 % of the field) to M-H-M (nobody) — a trade-off the ablation quantifies; down-weighting instead of dropping does not rescue it (tested: 0.112). MixedLM range gate replaced by the stint-fixed-effects baseline gates (`model_fallback.stint_fe_baseline`): the two fail only at Australia, whose practice shows no net degradation. Pace calibration now iterates to a fixed point at a stint length inside every compound's life (cycle-2 fix of an oscillation at Barcelona).

## 2. Two engineering cycles (say this in the report)

Cycle 1 (frozen under `bench/v3_cycle1/`) used an unconditional first-stop prior and the original pace-calibration passes. Its benchmark exposed three defects: (a) the prior mixed one- and two-stop first stops — at Belgium the 300-draw ablation search flipped to a 2-stop M-S-S @12 nobody ran while the 500-draw pipeline barely held the 1-stop; (b) the pace calibration oscillated at Barcelona (calibrated at 16.5 laps, plan at 24) and failed the V2 ladder regression test; (c) a held-out Barcelona MEDIUM budget of 3.27 s (a foreign lower bound below the prior) distorted its ladder. Cycle 2 fixed all three (stop-count conditioning; fixed-point calibration capped at 0.9 × the shortest compound life; bound-can-only-raise estimator with the consistency filter) and is the run reported.

Cycle 3 (the reported run) changed one thing after cycle 2's benchmark: the calibration sweeps choose the **smallest grid value within one weekend's worth of the best objective** instead of the argmin (one lap on one donor for the first-stop objective; one donor flipping one flag, 0.25/n, for the shape objective). Cycle 2's Australia-held-out block had walked τ to the new top of its grid (8.0, a 0.02 gain over τ = 4-6 on a plateau) and that τ, against a mapped Melbourne prior in which the 2023 one-stops no longer count, flipped Australia from the 1-stop M-H (53 % of the field) to a 2-stop M-H-H (7 %) - a stop-count regression the brief says must not happen. V2 had already described τ as saturating; the parsimonious end of a plateau is the honest reading of a constant the data do not identify. The rule is applied uniformly to λ, κ, τ and the grid penalty; report the constants it produced from the final `calibration.json`.

## 3. First-stop timing — the honest answer (the report's central question)

Cycle 1: mean |first stop − field| on the three non-SC weekends 5.0 (V2) → 4.33; cycle 2: see the final JSON. The λ×κ grid (`bench/out/firststop_grid.json`) shows why the ≤2-lap target is not reached: κ moves Hungary onto the field's lap (history mode 18 vs field 19) but at Barcelona and Austria no weight moves the first stop below the circuit's own historical mode (17; 21-25) while the 2026 field stopped at 13 and 18, earlier than any historical year. The only grid point under 2 laps (λ 0.6, κ 0) gets there by switching Austria and Hungary onto SOFT-start families (S-M-H) run by 1 of 17 and 0 of 19 finishers. The residual is between the circuit's history and the 2026 field, not in the objective. Next step: a degradation-aware prior (scale the historical first stop by the season/circuit degradation ratio, e.g. Barcelona's 2026/2025 race rate is 2.1×) — a rough check gives 14 at Barcelona but worsens Hungary; needs its own leave-one-out calibration.

## 4. Shipping decisions

* Ship: nomination mapping (Barcelona 0 % → 46 % of the field; prior-only outlook modal match 0 → 3/7 by identical code), forecast-only regime + circuit prior (pooled MAE, Belgium 0.052 → 0.029, coverage into the target band), first-stop prior (moves the timing by 0.7-1 lap and never breaks a family in the shipped run; Australia's 1-stop no longer pulled early after the unseen-family rule), per-circuit dirty air (neutral on decisions, physically right), stint-FE gate, cliff detector + box-now alerts (the wear alarm preceded 0/46 stops; the collapse signal is rare but real), fixed-point pace calibration.
* Ship with a caveat: the Melbourne ladder rule (fixes Australia's negative net and gate; costs Hungary's sequence). The alternative — keeping noisy pre-2026 nets — reproduces V2's negative net at Australia. Report both and recommend a per-circuit decision rule (drop only where a 2026 self-measurement disagrees in sign) as next work.
* Do not claim: per-car intelligence as a stint-ranking improvement (it is not); grip budgets as measurements (they remain lower bounds; 4 collapses in 339 stints); a ≤2-lap first-stop error.

## 5. Remaining failures / operational notes

* Gates: Australia's two stint-FE gates (practice shows no net degradation — genuine), Belgium's 103-clean-laps count gate (pre-existing), Barcelona's stint-length gate if it recurs (check the final meta), Italy's divergence if any.
* Hungary M-H-M (nobody ran it) — the ladder-rule trade-off above.
* Live tick rose ~70 → ~80 ms (collapse detector + first-stop term per car per lap); still 2.5× under the 200 ms target.
* Share of first stops inside the model's window fell (windows are ±2-3 laps and centred on the prior's mode).
* New-circuit season pool (Madring, the next race): mapping C4 MEDIUMs from C3/C4/C5 circuits onto Madring's C4 SOFT leans the prior-only start toward SOFT (65/96); the sealed FP1+FP2 fit dominates there, but the fallback should learn the role effect. Not benchmarked (Madring has no race yet).
* `regime_history` cache: Melbourne has no circuit regime prior (2024's ratio out of band, 2025 wet).
